Working Papers
======

**When Inflation Turns Volatile: Diverging Expectations of Households and Professionals** [[WP](https://tsunghsien1124.github.io/assets/files/CCL_inflation_expectations.pdf)]  
with [Nan-Kuang Chen](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~nankuang/) and [Tsung-Hsien Li](https://tsunghsien1124.github.io/)

**Abstract:** Are professional inflation forecasts more accurate than household expectations, and do the two groups form expectations differently across inflation environments? Using the MSC, the SPF, and the Livingston Survey over 1981–2024, we identify stable and volatile inflation regimes with a two-state Markov-switching model of realized CPI inflation. We find no significant difference in forecast accuracy, overall or within regimes. Expectation formation, however, differs. In stable periods both groups place similar weight on recent inflation, but when inflation becomes volatile, households place more weight on it while professionals place less. Central banks may therefore gain from monitoring both measures when inflation is volatile.

**Who Shows Up: Unemployment Insurance and the Composition of Job Seekers** [Draft available upon request]  
with [Tsung-Hsien Li](https://tsunghsien1124.github.io/)

**Abstract:** Debates over extending unemployment benefits ask whether the unemployed search less, but not whom firms meet when they post a vacancy. We build a continuous-time heterogeneous-agent search model with skill depreciation, endogenous search effort, and benefit exhaustion, and feed it the 2008–2013 U.S. federal benefit extensions. The effect on the applicants a vacancy meets changes sign over the program. Firms meet better applicants in 2008–09, because low-skill recipients cut search first; worse applicants from 2010 on, because those kept unemployed lose skill; and the worst applicants in the month the program expires. A new employer-side series turns at the same dates. Throughout, the extensions change the unemployed much more than the applicants firms meet.

Master's Thesis
======

**The Impact of Unemployment Benefits in an Economy with Search Frictions and Unemployment-Induced Productivity Loss** [[WP](https://thesis.dial.uclouvain.be/server/api/core/bitstreams/cf744eb9-9afb-4c8e-a1e0-a1d4a751f674/content)]  
Université catholique de Louvain, 2025

**Abstract:** This paper examines the impact of unemployment benefits on an economy characterized by search frictions in the labor market and unemployment-induced productivity loss on ex ante homogeneous workers facing idiosyncratic unemployment risks by building an extension on Krusell et al. (2010). The analysis is framed as a continuous-time dynamic programming problem, with the solution implemented using the finite-difference method outlined in Achdou et al. (2022). The results indicate that increasing unemployment benefits consistently improves welfare compared to the baseline scenario with a 1% payroll tax. However, this welfare improvement is accompanied by a significant rise in the variances of individual asset holdings and consumption levels. Furthermore, the analysis reveals that in the extension incorporating unemployment-induced productivity loss, firm’s profits and filled job values are higher compared to the original Krusell et al. (2010) framework.
